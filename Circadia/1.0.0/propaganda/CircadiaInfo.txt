Circadia is an astronomical watch face that shows the sky in real time based on your location. The 12 o'clock position is your northern horizon, with the center directly overhead. Face north, and the sky on your watch matches the sky above you.

As the sun sets each day, its halo shifts from yellow to red, reaching purple at nautical twilight, then blue by astronomical twilight. Full night begins only once the halo has completely passed below the horizon. Stars fade in through nautical twilight, reaching peak brightness by full night. At sunrise, the transition reverses. The minute digits take on the halo's color, while the hours flare orange through sunset and sunrise. The moon shows its current phase and position, with the illuminated side always facing the sun — just as it does in the real sky.

Circadia is mathematically detailed but visually quiet — a clean, minimal watch face with the sky drifting by underneath. It's a "normal" watch face first, but one that reminds you to look up sometimes, and to notice the rhythms behind the hours and minutes: the path of the sun, the phases of the moon, and the turning of the stars.

Currently supports AMOLED devices. MIP support and additional options planned for future release.

This is Circadia, an astronomical watch face I just released [on Connect IQ.]

\textbf{How it works:} The sun and moon move in real time based on your location. The 12 o'clock position is your northern horizon, with the centre directly overhead. Face north, and the sky on your watch matches the sky above you. For example, the video clip shows what my sky looked like the day of the last full moon. 

\textbf{What makes it different:} Most astronomical faces feel a bit cluttered or cartoonish to me. As somebody who prefers simpler faces, I wanted this to be mathematically detailed (see below!) but visually quiet. It's meant to be a "normal" watch face first, but one that reminds you that the sun is setting, or shows you where to look to see the moon. 

\textbf{Astronomy details:} As the sun sets, its 18-degree halo shifts from yellow to red, reaching purple at nautical twilight, then blue by astronomical twilight. Full night begins once the halo is completely below the horizon. Stars gradually appear from the end of civil twilight, reaching peak density by full night. At sunrise, the transition reverses. The minute digits take on the halo's colour, while the hour digits briefly flare orange through sunset and sunrise. The moon shows its current phase and position, with the illuminated part always facing the sun. The lunar halo appears within about a day of the full moon, reaching its maximum size at peak fullness. (I was lucky enough for this to happen during my nighttime on May 1, hence the giant halo!)

\textbf{Caveats:} This is my first watch face. I've tested it on FR265S and Vivoactive 5, but there are probably weird bugs I haven't hit. Feedback is very welcome, including suggestions for additional options and data fields. Currently released for AMOLED devices, with MIP support planned once I can test it.
