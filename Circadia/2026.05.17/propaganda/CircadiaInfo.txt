[Circadia][link] shows the positions of the sun and moon in real time, based on your location. The 12 o'clock position is your northern horizon, with the centre directly overhead. Face north, and the sky on your watch matches the sky above you. 

\textbf{What makes it different:} Most astronomical faces feel a bit cluttered or cartoonish to me. As somebody who prefers simpler faces, I wanted this to be mathematically detailed (see below!) but visually quiet. It's meant to be a "normal" watch face first, but one that reminds you that the sun is setting, or that the moon is full. 

\textbf{Astronomy details:} Relative to a typical watch size, the actual sun would span about a pixel. Boring! Instead, the displayed solar halo is 18 degrees in angular radius, chosen so that the transition between astronomical twilight and true night occurs just as the edge of the halo passes from sight. 

The halo's colour evolves through the twilight phases: yellow to red through sunset, red to purple through civil twilight, purple to blue through nautical, then drifting offscreen as astronomical twilight ends. The minute digits take on the halo's colour, and the hour digits briefly flare orange through sunset and sunrise. Stars begin appearing at the end of civil twilight, reaching peak density by true night. 

The moon shows its current phase and position, with the lit side always facing the sun. The lunar halo appears within about a day of the full moon, reaching its maximum size at the moment of peak fullness. 

\textbf{Caveats:} This is my first watch face. I've tested it on FR265S and Vivoactive 5, but there are probably weird bugs I haven't hit. Feedback is very welcome, including suggestions for additional options and data fields. Currently released for AMOLED devices, with MIP support planned once I can test it.
