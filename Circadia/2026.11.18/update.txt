Changes since 2026.11.17:
The sun's visible edges at sunset now set at twilight boundaries: the core (12°) at the end of nautical twilight, the halo (18°) at the end of astronomical twilight (full night). The big white disc is kept at 10°.
Moon sizes follow the white disc: Small 15°, Medium 17.5°, Large 20° (the largest matches the white disc exactly).
MIP: a ball the size of the white disc (10°) and two thin rings, to 12° and 14°. Through twilight the outer ring widens to 18°, so the MIP glow also ends exactly at full night (-18°); the inner ring sets at the end of nautical twilight.
AMOLED: a stronger sun halo. The white centre now stays until golden hour and fades during it (+6° to sunset), straight into the sun's yellow-to-orange shift, instead of first turning flat yellow. On MIP the white ball lasts until +3°.
AMOLED: softer sun edges. The white is pure to 9° and eases into the sun's colour by 11° (half white at the 10° disc edge), then a ring of full colour to the core's edge at 12°. The halo now leaves the core smoothly, with no step, and bends onto the previous straight fade by 14°; its 20% outer edge at 18° is unchanged.
The horizon glow is now built in and the setting is gone. From the end of golden hour (+6°) the sun itself spreads along the horizon, on AMOLED and MIP: every layer keeps its top edge in place while its curve flattens, goes straight and then hugs the edge. The sun's outer edge is straight at -3°. After that it wraps further and further along the horizon: its outer edge meets the rim ±60° either side of the sun's bearing at the end of nautical twilight, and ±90° (half the horizon) as it sets at full night.
