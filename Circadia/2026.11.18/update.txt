Changes since 2026.11.17:
Every edge of the AMOLED sun now leaves the sky at a twilight boundary: white disc 6° (end of civil twilight), core 12° (end of nautical), halo 18° (end of astronomical). MIP keeps a compact sun: ball 6°, rings 9° and 12°, so it sets at the end of nautical twilight.
Moon sizes follow: the largest moon (12°) matches the sun's white disc; Small and Medium keep their old proportions (9° and 10.5°).
AMOLED: a stronger sun halo.
Horizon glow on (AMOLED and MIP): there is no separate glow any more. From the end of golden hour (+6°) the sun itself spreads along the horizon: every layer keeps its top edge in place while its curve flattens, goes straight and then hugs the edge. The outer halo is straight at -3°; the spread is greatest at the end of nautical twilight. With the setting off the sun stays round.
On MIP the ball and both rings spread the same way, so the MIP glow ends with its sun at -12°.
