Changes since 2026.11.18:
Real stars. The sky now shows the actual brightest stars, from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, exactly where they are for your location and time: Orion rises in the east on winter evenings, the Plough circles the pole, and so on. "Some" shows the 100 brightest stars above your horizon at full night, "Lots" the 200 brightest.
New setting, "Which stars": Brightest (the brightest stars, as above), Constellations or Zodiac. Constellations favours the shapes people know: the Big and Little Dippers, Cassiopeia's W, Orion, the Summer Triangle, the Northern Cross, the Square of Pegasus, the Teapot, the Southern Cross, Scorpius, the Pleiades and more, each shown whole. Very bright stars still make the cut. In this mode, at full night, faint deep-blue lines draw the well-known constellations in their usual star-chart figures (Orion with his club and shield, the Great Bear, Scorpius...). The lines all come out together when full night begins and all go when it ends. Figures are always drawn complete: a figure low in the sky simply runs off the edge of the sky, as in a planetarium.
Star colours and sizes are real too: each star's colour comes from its measured colour (blue Rigel, white Vega, amber Arcturus, red Betelgeuse), and its size from its brightness. Each star keeps its size and colour all night.
Stars pop in as they would to the eye: the brightest first, a few degrees after sunset; the brightest dozen or so by the end of civil twilight; nearly all by the end of nautical twilight; and the last faint ones low near the horizon, where the air dims them, by full night. At dawn they leave in reverse.
Zodiac shows the twelve zodiac constellations with their lines at full night, from Aries to Pisces, along with the brightest stars to fill the sky to your chosen number of stars.
The sun now keeps its round shape for longer: it starts to spread along the horizon just before sunset (+3°) and flattens gradually to a straight edge by the end of civil twilight (-6°), before wrapping along the horizon as before.
