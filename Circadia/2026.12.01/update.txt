Changes since 2026.11.18:
Real stars. The sky now shows the actual brightest stars, from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, exactly where they are for your location and time: Orion rises in the east on winter evenings, the Plough circles the pole, and so on. At full night the sky holds the 200 brightest stars above your horizon. The "Number of stars" setting is gone: the sky is now built a little at a time, so the full sky is safe on every watch (watches with less memory show 100).
New setting, "Which stars": Brightest (the brightest stars, as above), Constellations or Zodiac. Constellations favours the shapes people know: the Big and Little Dippers, Cassiopeia's W, Orion, the Summer Triangle, the Northern Cross, the Square of Pegasus, the Teapot, the Southern Cross, Scorpius, the Pleiades and more, each shown whole. Very bright stars still make the cut. In this mode, at full night, quiet blue lines draw the well-known constellations as on the IAU's official star charts (Orion with his club and shield, the Great Bear with its paws, Scorpius...), with Leo kept in its familiar Sickle-and-body shape and Sagittarius drawn as the whole archer, not just the Teapot. The lines all come out together when full night begins and all go when it ends. Figures are always drawn complete: a figure low in the sky simply runs off the edge of the sky, as in a planetarium.
New: the bright planets. Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn appear with the stars, exactly where they are in the sky: small round discs, a touch brighter than the stars, in their own colours (cream-white Venus, red-orange Mars, peach Jupiter, gold Saturn with a little ring). They come out by their real brightness, so Venus shows just after sunset and Jupiter soon after, before any star.
Star colours and sizes are real too: each star's colour comes from its measured colour (blue Rigel, white Vega, amber Arcturus, red Betelgeuse), and its size from its brightness. Each star keeps its size and colour all night. The brightest stars are now drawn round.
Stars pop in as they would to the eye: the brightest first, a few degrees after sunset; the brightest dozen or so by the end of civil twilight; nearly all by the end of nautical twilight; and the last faint ones low near the horizon, where the air dims them, by full night. At dawn they leave in reverse.
Zodiac shows the twelve zodiac constellations with their lines at full night, from Aries to Pisces, along with the brightest stars to fill the sky.
The sun now keeps its round shape for longer: it starts to spread along the horizon just before sunset (+3°) and flattens gradually to a straight edge by the end of civil twilight (-6°), before wrapping along the horizon as before.
"Moon size" is now "Sun and moon size": the sun's white disc always matches the moon, and the whole sun scales with it (Large is the full-size sun). Smaller suns keep glowing at the horizon until full night, so twilight is always told by the sun's colour.
MIP: a new sun. Its colours now flow as it goes down: each new colour grows in at the edge and pushes the one inside it down, while the brightest colours sink below the horizon. It follows the AMOLED sun stage by stage, from yellow by day to amber at sunset, red at the end of civil twilight, purple at the end of nautical twilight and blue at full night, with no sudden colour switches.
AMOLED: the moon's dark side is a little brighter.
Settings are reordered: the sky first (Which stars, the moon's dark side), then the clock and data fields, and the rarely changed ones (Sun and moon size, minute colour saturation, units, location) at the bottom. Your saved choices are kept.
Fixed: on some watches (such as the Forerunner 165) a coloured bar could cross the screen near the end of civil twilight.
The "Display type" setting is gone: Circadia now picks its art from the watch itself, full colour on AMOLED screens and the simplified art on MIP screens.
Now available on more MIP watches: fēnix 7, 7S, 7X and 7 Pro series (with quatix 7, tactix 7 and Enduro 2), Forerunner 255 series and 955.
